## Module 1: Foundations & Scientific Methods
*Goal: Understand the history, perspectives, and empirical methods that define psychology as a science.*

### Lesson 1.1: Historical Perspectives & Subfields
* **Concept:** Structuralism (Wundt, Titchener) vs. Functionalism (James); Behavioral, Cognitive, Biological, Evolutionary, Psychodynamic, Humanistic, and Sociocultural perspectives.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How analyzing a patient with anxiety differs when viewed by a behaviorist (reward/fear pairing) vs. a neuroscientist (serotonin deficiency).
* **Interactive Target:** `perspective-categorizer`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain why modern psychology uses multiple perspectives to analyze human behavior rather than just one."

### Lesson 1.2: Descriptive & Experimental Research Methods
* **Concept:** Naturalistic observation, case studies, surveys (wording effects); Correlational studies vs. Experiments (IV, DV, confounding variables, experimental/control groups, random selection vs. random assignment).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Why a pharmaceutical trial requires random assignment to isolate drug efficacy from placebo effects.
* **Interactive Target:** `experiment-designer`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the difference between a correlational study and an experiment, and why only experiments can establish causation."

### Lesson 1.3: Statistics & Research Ethics
* **Concept:** Descriptive (mean, median, mode, standard deviation, normal curve) vs. Inferential statistics (statistical significance, p-values, Type I/II errors); Ethical guidelines (IRB, informed consent, deception, debriefing, animal welfare).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Deciphering what "p < 0.05" actually means in a journal reporting a new treatment for depression.
* **Interactive Target:** `descriptive-stats-bell-curve`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Describe what statistical significance means to a researcher, and outline the ethical precautions required in studies involving human deception."

## Module 2: Biological Bases of Behavior
*Goal: Master the biological mechanisms that control neural communication, brain structures, and genetic heritability.*

### Lesson 2.1: Neural Anatomy & Synaptic Transmission
* **Concept:** Structure of a neuron (dendrites, soma, axon, myelin sheath, terminal buttons); Action potential (sodium-potassium pump, depolarization, all-or-none response); Synaptic transmission (neurotransmitter release, vesicle binding, reuptake).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How demyelination of axons in Multiple Sclerosis disrupts sensory and motor signaling.
* **Interactive Target:** `axon-firing-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain how an electrical impulse travels down a neuron and converts into a chemical message at the synapse."

### Lesson 2.2: Neurochemistry: Agonists & Antagonists
* **Concept:** Major neurotransmitters (Acetylcholine, Dopamine, Serotonin, Norepinephrine, GABA, Glutamate, Endorphins); Drug mechanisms of action (agonists mimicking vs. antagonists blocking receptors).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How L-Dopa treats Parkinson's by boosting dopamine, while curare blocks acetylcholine receptors causing paralysis.
* **Interactive Target:** `neurotransmitter-synapse-matcher`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the difference between a neurotransmitter agonist and antagonist, and give an example of how each affects synaptic transmission."

### Lesson 2.3: Nervous & Endocrine Systems
* **Concept:** Central (CNS) vs. Peripheral (PNS); Somatic vs. Autonomic divisions; Sympathetic vs. Parasympathetic pathways; Endocrine system (glands, hormones, pituitary "master gland").
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** The rapid physiological shifts (racing heart, stopped digestion) during a near-miss car accident vs. endocrine adjustments of adrenaline.
* **Interactive Target:** `fight-or-flight-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Contrast the physiological changes triggered by the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems during and after an emergency."

### Lesson 2.4: Brain Structure & Neuroplasticity
* **Concept:** Brain stem (medulla, pons, reticular formation), Cerebellum; Limbic system (thalamus, hypothalamus, amygdala, hippocampus); Cerebral cortex lobes, motor/somatosensory strips, association areas, Broca's/Wernicke's; Neuroplasticity and split-brain research.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Phineas Gage's personality shift following frontal lobe damage, and how split-brain patients read words processed by separate hemispheres.
* **Interactive Target:** `interactive-brain-explorer`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Detail the specialized roles of the limbic system structures and explain how split-brain patients help us study hemisphere lateralization."

### Lesson 2.5: Brain Imaging & Study Methods
* **Concept:** Study methods (lesions, electrical stimulation); Structural imaging (CT, MRI) vs. Functional imaging (EEG, PET, fMRI).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Deciding whether to scan a patient with a suspected brain tumor using a structural MRI or tracking seizure activity with an EEG.
* **Interactive Target:** `brain-imaging-scanner`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Describe how an fMRI scan differs from an EEG and standard MRI, and when a researcher would choose each."

## Module 3: Sensation, Perception, & Consciousness
*Goal: Explore how physical energy is transducer into psychological sensations, how we organize this input, and how our conscious states shift.*

### Lesson 3.1: Sensory Mechanisms & Vision
* **Concept:** Sensation vs. Perception, transduction, thresholds (absolute/JND), Weber's Law, sensory adaptation; Visual anatomy (cornea, pupil, lens, retina, rods/cones, optic nerve, visual cortex); Dual-process color vision (Trichromatic vs. Opponent-Process).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Staring at a green, black, and yellow flag and seeing a red, white, and blue afterimage due to opponent-process adaptation.
* **Interactive Target:** `opponent-process-afterimage`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain transduction in the eye, and discuss how the Trichromatic and Opponent-Process theories together explain color vision."

### Lesson 3.2: Audition, Somatosensation, & Chemical Senses
* **Concept:** Auditory anatomy (cochlea, basilar membrane, hair cells), pitch theories (Place vs. Frequency); Touch, temperature, Gate-Control theory of pain; Taste, olfaction; Vestibular (balance) and Kinesthetic (body position) senses.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How rubbing a stubbed toe stimulates large sensory fibers to close the spinal gate to pain signals.
* **Interactive Target:** `gate-control-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the Gate-Control theory of pain and describe the differences between the vestibular and kinesthetic senses."

### Lesson 3.3: Perceptual Organization & Interpretation
* **Concept:** Gestalt principles of grouping (proximity, similarity, continuity, closure, figure-ground); Depth perception (monocular vs. binocular cues); Perceptual constancies, top-down vs. bottom-up processing, perceptual sets; Cross-cultural carpentered-world contrasts.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Why individuals in carpentered cities are highly susceptible to the Müller-Lyer illusion, whereas those from rural non-carpentered cultures are not.
* **Interactive Target:** `gestalt-shape-matcher`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the difference between bottom-up and top-down processing, and give a real-world example of how a perceptual set influences what we perceive."

### Lesson 3.4: Attention & Sensory Interaction
* **Concept:** Selective attention (cocktail party effect), inattentional blindness, change blindness; Sensory interaction (McGurk Effect, Synesthesia, Stroop Effect).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Failing to notice a person in a gorilla suit walking across a basketball court because attention is focused on counting passes.
* **Interactive Target:** `stroop-attention-tester`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the difference between selective and divided attention, and describe how inattentional blindness occurs."

### Lesson 3.5: Sleep, Dreams, & States of Consciousness
* **Concept:** Circadian rhythms, sleep stages (NREM-1, NREM-2 [sleep spindles, K-complexes], NREM-3 [delta waves], REM sleep); sleep disorders (insomnia, narcolepsy, apnea, night terrors); Dream theories (Freudian, activation-synthesis, cognitive development).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Why waking up during REM sleep leaves us with vivid recollections of dreams, and how sleep apnea interrupts deep delta sleep.
* **Interactive Target:** `sleep-cycle-hypnogram`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Describe the electrical and physiological changes that occur as a person transitions from awake to NREM-3 sleep, and into REM sleep."

### Lesson 3.6: Psychoactive Drugs & Neurochemistry
* **Concept:** Depressants (alcohol, opiates), Stimulants (nicotine, cocaine, amphetamines), Hallucinogens (LSD, THC); Neurochemical mechanisms at the synapse; Tolerance, withdrawal, physical vs. psychological dependence, addiction.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How cocaine blocks the reuptake of dopamine, leading to an intense high followed by a depressive crash as receptors deplete.
* **Interactive Target:** `drug-synaptic-effects-lab`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Classify the major groups of psychoactive drugs, and explain how tolerance and withdrawal develop neurochemically."

## Module 5: Learning & Memory
*Goal: Explore the behavioral, cognitive, and social mechanisms of learning, alongside the structures and fallibility of human memory.*

### Lesson 5.1: Classical Conditioning
* **Concept:** Pavlovian conditioning; UCS, UCR, CS, CR; Acquisition, extinction, spontaneous recovery, generalization, discrimination; Biological constraints (John Garcia's conditioned taste aversion / preparedness).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How chemotherapy patients develop a nausea response to the waiting room before receiving treatment, and why rats easily pair taste with illness but not light with illness.
* **Interactive Target:** `classical-salivation-lab`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain how a neutral stimulus becomes a conditioned stimulus, and how John Garcia's taste aversion studies challenged behaviorism."

### Lesson 5.2: Operant Conditioning
* **Concept:** Thorndike's Law of Effect, Skinner's operant conditioning, Skinner box; Reinforcement vs. Punishment (positive vs. negative); Schedules of reinforcement (FR, VR, FI, VI); Shaping, primary vs. secondary reinforcers.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Why slot machines (variable-ratio reinforcement) create highly addictive, extinction-resistant gambling behaviors compared to vending machines (continuous reinforcement).
* **Interactive Target:** `skinner-box-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Differentiate between negative reinforcement and positive punishment, and explain why variable-ratio schedules yield high response rates and resistance to extinction."

### Lesson 5.3: Cognitive & Social Learning
* **Concept:** Bandura's observational learning (Bobo Doll, modeling); Tolman's latent learning and cognitive maps; Köhler's insight learning; Seligman's learned helplessness.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Why children who watch an adult beat up an inflatable doll mimic those exact aggressive behaviors, and how a rat explores a maze without rewards to form a mental map.
* **Interactive Target:** `latent-learning-maze`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the concepts of latent learning and learned helplessness, and explain how they demonstrate that learning involves cognitive processes, not just associations."

### Lesson 5.4: Memory Models & Storage Pathways
* **Concept:** Atkinson-Shiffrin model (sensory [iconic/echoic], STM/working memory [chunking, mnemonics], LTM); Explicit/declarative (semantic, episodic) vs. Implicit/non-declarative (procedural, priming); Memory brain structures (hippocampus, cerebellum, basal ganglia, amygdala, Long-Term Potentiation LTP).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Patient H.M., who lost his hippocampus and could not form new explicit memories (episodic), but could still learn new motor skills (implicit).
* **Interactive Target:** `memory-span-tester`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Describe the capacity and duration of the three memory stages in the Atkinson-Shiffrin model, and identify the brain structures associated with explicit vs. implicit memory."

### Lesson 5.5: Forgetting & Memory Construction
* **Concept:** Retrieval failure, storage decay, encoding failure; Interference (proactive vs. retroactive); Amnesia (anterograde vs. retrograde); Memory construction, Loftus's misinformation effect, source amnesia, eyewitness reliability.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How changing a verb in a question from "hit" to "smashed" leads eyewitnesses to recall seeing broken glass at a car accident when none was present.
* **Interactive Target:** `eyewitness-testimony-bias`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the difference between proactive and retroactive interference, and describe how the misinformation effect demonstrates the reconstructive nature of memory."

## Module 6: Cognition, Motivation, & Emotion

* **Interactive Target:** `heuristic-bias-evaluator`

### Lesson 6.3: Motivation: Drives, Hunger, & Hypothalamic Systems
* **Concept:** Theories of motivation (instinct, drive-reduction [homeostasis], optimum arousal [Yerkes-Dodson], Maslow's hierarchy); Physiology of hunger (lateral hypothalamus [LH] vs. ventromedial hypothalamus [VMH], metabolic hormones: ghrelin, leptin, insulin); Intrinsic vs. Extrinsic motivation.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How damage to a rat's lateral hypothalamus leads to starvation (aphagia), while damage to the ventromedial hypothalamus leads to severe overeating (hyperphagia).

### Lesson 6.4: Theories of Emotion & Stress Adaptation

* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Spotting a bear in the woods: do you run and then feel afraid (James-Lange), or does your physiological response combine with your cognitive appraisal to create fear (Two-Factor)?

### Lesson 6.5: Social Motivation & Achievement
