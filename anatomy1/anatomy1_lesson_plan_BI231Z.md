## Module 1: Foundational Frameworks & Chemistry
*Goal: Master levels of organization, homeostatic loops, chemical bonds, pH buffering, transport, and tissue layers.*

### Lesson 1.1: Foundational Frameworks & Homeostasis
* **Concept:** Levels of biological organization (chemical, cellular, tissue, organ, organ system, organismal). Anatomical position, directional terms, body planes and sections, body cavities, abdominopelvic quadrants and regions. Overview of the 11 organ systems of the human body (name, major organs, primary functions). Mechanics of negative and positive feedback loops (sensor, control center, effector) and feedback failure.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How the body responds to a heat stroke emergency (hyperthermia) by dilating blood vessels and sweating, and why feedback failure causes organ damage.
* **Interactive Target:** `homeostatic-temperature-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain how homeostatic negative feedback loops differ from positive feedback loops, outline the levels of biological organization, and summarize the primary functions of the 11 organ systems."

### Lesson 1.2: General & Inorganic Chemistry
* **Concept:** Matter, elements, and atoms. Structure of an atom (protons, neutrons, electrons) and isotopes. Chemical bonding: ionic, covalent, polar covalent, and hydrogen bonds. Inorganic compounds: properties of water (polarity, solvency, high heat capacity, cohesion). Acids, bases, pH scale, and chemical buffer systems (bicarbonate buffer).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How an intravenous infusion of unbuffered solution causes immediate blood pH shifts, and how the bicarbonate buffer system prevents sudden systemic shock.
* **Interactive Target:** `inorganic-chemical-buffer-balancer`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Differentiate between ionic, covalent, and hydrogen bonds, explain the unique properties of water that support life, and describe how chemical buffer systems maintain pH stability."

### Lesson 1.3: Organic Biochemistry & Enzymes
* **Concept:** Organic Biochemistry Primer: Structure and physiological roles of Carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, and nucleic acids. Enzyme kinetics: activation energy, substrate specificity, active site conformation, Vmax, and Km. ATP production foundations (cellular energy transfer).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How metabolic acidosis alters enzyme conformation, causing systemic shock, and how restoring physiological pH restores active site geometry.
* **Interactive Target:** `biochemical-kinetic-solver`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain how enzymes speed up chemical reactions, what factors influence enzyme kinetics (pH, temperature, concentrations), and detail the chemical structure and physiological role of the four major organic macromolecules."

### Lesson 1.4: Cell Membrane Transport & Cell Division
* **Concept:** Cell membrane structure (phospholipid bilayer, proteins). Membrane transport mechanisms: passive (simple/facilitated diffusion, osmosis, tonicity) vs. active transport (primary/secondary active transport, vesicular transport). Resting membrane potentials. Protein synthesis (transcription in nucleus, translation at ribosome). DNA replication mechanics (helicase, DNA polymerase, leading/lagging strand). Cell cycle phases (interphase [G1, S, G2], mitosis stages [prophase, metaphase, anaphase, telophase], cytokinesis) and cell cycle checkpoints. Cell junctions (tight junctions, desmosomes, gap junctions).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Why infusing pure water intravenously is fatal due to tonicity shifts, and how defective chloride transport channels cause cystic fibrosis.
* **Interactive Target:** `membrane-transport-sorter`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Detail how cell membrane transport systems operate, explaining the differences between active and passive transport, outline the stages of protein synthesis (transcription and translation), summarize the mechanics of DNA replication and the stages of the cell cycle/mitosis, and explain the structure and function of the three major cell junctions."

### Lesson 1.5: Histology (The 4 Tissue Types)
* **Concept:** Structural characteristics, locations, and functions of Epithelial, Connective, Muscle, and Nervous tissues. Body membranes (epithelial: mucous, serous, cutaneous; connective: synovial), glandular epithelium (endocrine vs. exocrine), and exocrine secretion mechanisms (merocrine, apocrine, holocrine). Physiology of tissue repair (regeneration vs. fibrosis) and the effects of aging on tissues.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Under a digital microscope, visually differentiating transitional epithelium (bladder) from stratified squamous epithelium (skin) to identify cancer margins or bladder dysfunction, and evaluating scarring (fibrosis) in post-infarct tissues.
* **Interactive Target:** `digital-microscope-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Differentiate the structural matrices, locations, and physiological roles of the four primary tissue types, contrast mucous, serous, and synovial membranes, explain exocrine secretions (merocrine, apocrine, holocrine), and describe the difference between tissue regeneration and fibrosis."

## Module 2: Protective Frameworks & Structural Support
*Goal: Understand the integumentary protective barrier, skeletal structures, and joint kinematics.*

### Lesson 1.6: Integumentary System & Wound Healing
* **Concept:** Strata of the epidermis (basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum, corneum), cellular composition (keratinocytes, melanocytes, Merkel cells, Langerhans cells), dermal layers (papillary, reticular), accessory structures (hair, glands), and the physiology of deep wound healing (hemostasis, inflammatory, proliferative, remodeling). Burn assessment via Rule of Nines and skin cancer tracking via ABCDE criteria.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Using the Rule of Nines to calculate burn fluid resuscitation and mapping suspicious moles using the ABCDE criteria to track skin cancer.
* **Interactive Target:** `rule-of-nines-burn-tracker`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Detail the layers and cellular composition of the epidermis and dermis, describe the physiological sequence of deep wound healing, and explain how the Rule of Nines and ABCDE criteria are applied clinically."

### Lesson 1.7: Bone Tissue Physiology & Calcium Regulation
* **Concept:** Bone tissue histology (osteons, central canal, trabeculae, osteoblasts, osteoclasts, osteocytes), intramembranous and endochondral ossification, bone remodeling, calcium homeostasis (Ca2+ regulation via PTH and calcitonin), bone fracture classifications (simple, compound, comminuted, greenstick), and the four physiological stages of bone fracture repair.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Evaluating a patient with osteoporosis and describing how hyperactive osteoclast kinetics leads to fractures on a radiology scan.
* **Interactive Target:** `calcium-homeostasis-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Describe the histology of bone tissue, the process of endochondral ossification, the hormonal regulation of calcium homeostasis by PTH and calcitonin, and explain the physiological stages of bone fracture repair."

### Lesson 1.8: Axial Skeleton Landmarks
* **Concept:** Divisions of the skeleton (axial vs. appendicular overview). Bones and key landmarks of the axial skeleton: skull bones (cranial: frontal, parietal, temporal, occipital, sphenoid, ethmoid; facial: maxilla, mandible, zygomatic, nasal, lacrimal, palatine, vomer), vertebral column regions (cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacrum, coccyx) and curves, and thoracic cage (sternum, ribs: true, false, floating). Key markings (foramen magnum, sella turcica, external acoustic meatus, mandibular fossa).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Analyzing how a fracture of the ethmoid bone's cribriform plate can lead to a cerebrospinal fluid leak and loss of smell (anosmia) after head trauma.
* **Interactive Target:** `axial-landmarks-tagger`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Identify the primary bones of the skull, vertebral column, and thoracic cage, describe the regions and curvatures of the spine, and detail key markings such as the foramen magnum and sella turcica."

### Lesson 1.9: Appendicular Skeleton Landmarks
* **Concept:** Bones and key landmarks of the appendicular skeleton: pectoral girdle (clavicle, scapula), upper extremity (humerus, radius, ulna, carpals, metacarpals, phalanges), pelvic girdle (ilium, ischium, pubis), lower extremity (femur, patella, tibia, fibula, tarsals, metatarsals, phalanges). Key markings (glenoid cavity, acetabulum, greater/lesser trochanters, lateral/medial malleolus, tibial tuberosity).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Comparing the high range of motion but low stability of the scapular glenoid cavity with the deep, highly stable pelvic acetabulum in athletic injuries.
* **Interactive Target:** `appendicular-landmarks-tagger`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Outline the bones and joints comprising the pectoral and pelvic girdles and upper/lower extremities, and describe the functional significance of markings like the glenoid cavity and acetabulum."

### Lesson 1.10: Joints and Articulations
* **Concept:** Structural classification of joints (fibrous: sutures, syndesmoses, gomphoses; cartilaginous: synchondroses, symphyses; synovial: fluid-filled cavity) and functional classification (synarthrosis, amphiarthrosis, diarthrosis). Synovial joint anatomy (articular cartilage, joint capsule, synovial fluid, ligaments, bursae). Types of synovial joints (plane, hinge, pivot, condylar, saddle, ball-and-socket) and kinematic movements (flexion/extension, abduction/adduction, circumduction, rotation, pronation/supination, inversion/eversion, dorsiflexion/plantar flexion, elevation/depression).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Diagnosing a patient with an ACL tear vs. osteoarthritis of the knee to understand how ligamentous support and articular cartilage wear affect synovial joint kinematics.
* **Interactive Target:** `joint-kinematics-matcher`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Distinguish between fibrous, cartilaginous, and synovial joints structurally and functionally, describe the anatomy of a synovial joint, and outline the different synovial joint types and their movements."

## Module 3: Movement & Neurophysiology
*Goal: Master neuromuscular contraction physiology, skeletal muscle lever systems, glial cell support, and action potential biophysics.*

### Lesson 1.11: Muscle Contraction Physiology & Metabolism
* **Concept:** Microscopic anatomy of a sarcomere (actin, myosin, troponin, tropomyosin, Z-discs, A/I bands). Comparison of muscle tissue types (skeletal: striated, multinucleated, voluntary; cardiac: striated, branched, intercalated discs, involuntary; smooth: non-striated, spindle-shaped, involuntary). Events at the neuromuscular junction (NMJ), excitation-contraction coupling, sliding filament mechanism, and cross-bridge cycle. Muscle metabolism (creatine phosphate, anaerobic glycolysis, aerobic respiration) and muscle fiber types (slow oxidative [Type I], fast oxidative [Type IIa], fast glycolytic [Type IIb]). Muscle twitch mechanics (latent, contraction, relaxation phases), wave summation, tetanus, motor unit recruitment, and contractions (isotonic [concentric/eccentric] vs. isometric).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Analyzing how Botox blocks acetylcholine release at the NMJ causing flaccid paralysis vs. how tetanus toxin causes spastic paralysis (lockjaw), and tracing how muscular dystrophy alters dystrophin support.
* **Interactive Target:** `sarcomere-sequencing-game`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the chronological steps of excitation-contraction coupling and the sliding filament mechanism of muscle contraction, detail muscle twitch phases, compare the three muscle tissue types, and differentiate slow oxidative vs. fast glycolytic fibers."

### Lesson 1.12: Gross Muscular Anatomy & Lever Systems
* **Concept:** Muscle actions and relationships (agonists, antagonists, synergists, fixators). Naming rules for skeletal muscles (location, shape, relative size, fiber direction, number of origins, origin/insertion, action). Major skeletal muscle groups (facial, neck, abdominal, limb muscles: masseter, temporalis, deltoid, biceps brachii, rectus abdominis, rectus femoris, gastrocnemius, gluteus maximus) and their actions. Skeletal muscle lever systems (first, second, third class).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Analyzing how mastication (chewing) or calf extension leverages skeletal structures to generate force, and identifying muscle naming conventions to easily locate muscle groups in surgery.
* **Interactive Target:** `muscle-lever-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Identify the major skeletal muscles of the human body and their respective actions, explain agonist/antagonist muscle relationships, outline the rules for naming skeletal muscles, and analyze first, second, and third-class lever systems in the human body."

### Lesson 1.13: Nervous Tissue and Glial Cells
* **Concept:** General organization of the nervous system (CNS vs. PNS; sensory/afferent vs. motor/efferent divisions; somatic vs. autonomic nervous systems). Anatomy of a neuron (dendrites, soma, axon, axon hillock, myelin sheath, nodes of Ranvier). Classification of neurons (structural: multipolar, bipolar, unipolar; functional: sensory, motor, interneurons). Glial cells of the CNS (astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, ependymal cells) and PNS (Schwann cells, satellite cells). Myelination process.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Diagnosing a patient with multiple sclerosis to see how autoimmune destruction of CNS myelin slows nerve conduction, leading to sensory and motor deficits.
* **Interactive Target:** `glial-cell-sorter`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Describe the structural components of a neuron and classify neurons structurally and functionally, outline the divisions of the nervous system, and explain the location and roles of the six types of neuroglia."

### Lesson 1.14: Excitable Membranes & Action Potentials
* **Concept:** Membrane potentials (resting membrane potential: -70 mV, role of Na+/K+ pump and leak channels). Ion channels (leak, voltage-gated, ligand-gated, mechanically-gated). Graded potentials vs. action potentials. Biophysics of action potentials: threshold (-55 mV), depolarization (voltage-gated Na+ influx), repolarization (Na+ channel inactivation, voltage-gated K+ efflux), hyperpolarization, and refractory periods (absolute vs. relative). Synaptic transmission: electrical vs. chemical synapses, postsynaptic potentials (EPSPs vs. IPSPs), and synaptic integration (spatial and temporal summation at the axon hillock).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How altering extracellular potassium concentrations (hyperkalemia) blocks action potential propagation and causes cardiac arrest.
* **Interactive Target:** `action-potential-ion-grapher`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the biophysical mechanisms that generate graded potentials and action potentials, detailing the role of voltage-gated sodium and potassium channels, and explain how EPSPs and IPSPs undergo spatial and temporal summation."
