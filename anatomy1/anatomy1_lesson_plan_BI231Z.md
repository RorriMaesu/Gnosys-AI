## Module 1: Foundational Frameworks & Homeostasis
*Goal: Master anatomical position, feedback loops, directional terms, and biochemical cellular support.*

### Lesson 1.1: Foundational Frameworks & Homeostasis
* **Concept:** Anatomical position, directional terms, body planes and sections, body cavities, abdominopelvic quadrants and regions. Mechanics of homeostatic negative and positive feedback loops (sensor, control center, effector).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How the body responds to a heat stroke emergency (hyperthermia) by dilating blood vessels and sweating, and why feedback failure causes organ damage.
* **Interactive Target:** `homeostatic-temperature-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain how homeostatic negative feedback loops differ from positive feedback loops, and describe what happens to the body when feedback loops fail (e.g., during hyperthermia)."

### Lesson 1.2: Chemical & Cellular Level of Organization
* **Concept:** Membrane transport mechanisms (simple/facilitated diffusion, primary/secondary active transport, vesicular transport), resting membrane potentials, and cellular organelles. Organic Biochemistry Primer (macromolecules, enzyme kinetics, ATP production foundations, and cellular pH balancing).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Why infusing pure water intravenously is fatal due to tonicity shifts, and how defective chloride transport channels cause cystic fibrosis.
* **Interactive Target:** `membrane-transport-sorter`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Detail how cell membrane transport systems operate, highlighting the differences between active and passive transport, and explain the roles of enzyme kinematics and ATP in these mechanisms."

### Lesson 1.3: Histology (The 4 Tissue Types)
* **Concept:** Structural characteristics, locations, and functions of Epithelial, Connective, Muscle, and Nervous tissues.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Under a digital microscope, visually differentiating transitional epithelium (bladder) from stratified squamous epithelium (skin) to identify cancer margins or bladder dysfunction.
* **Interactive Target:** `digital-microscope-simulator`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Differentiate the structural matrices, locations, and physiological roles of the four primary tissue types in the human body."

## Module 2: Protective Frameworks & Structural Support
*Goal: Understand the integumentary protective barrier and skeletal kinematics.*

### Lesson 1.4: The Integumentary System
* **Concept:** Strata of the epidermis (basale, spinosum, granulosum, lucidum, corneum), cellular composition (keratinocytes, melanocytes, Merkel cells, Langerhans cells), dermal layers (papillary, reticular), accessory structures (hair, glands), and the physiology of deep wound healing (hemostasis, inflammatory, proliferative, remodeling).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Using the Rule of Nines to calculate burn fluid resuscitation and mapping suspicious moles using the ABCDE criteria to track skin cancer.
* **Interactive Target:** `rule-of-nines-burn-tracker`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Detail the layers and cellular composition of the epidermis and dermis, and describe the physiological sequence of deep wound healing."

### Lesson 1.5: The Skeletal System & Articulations
* **Concept:** Bone tissue histology (osteons, central canal, trabeculae), intramembranous and endochondral ossification, bone remodeling, calcium homeostasis (Ca2+ regulation via PTH and calcitonin), axial/appendicular landmarks, joint classifications (structural: fibrous, cartilaginous, synovial; functional: synarthrosis, amphiarthrosis, diarthrosis), synovial joint movements (circumduction, inversion, supination, pronation).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Evaluating a patient with osteoporosis and describing how hyperactive osteoclast kinetics leads to fractures on a radiology scan.
* **Interactive Target:** `bone-landmarks-tagger`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Describe the histology of bone tissue, the hormonal regulation of calcium homeostasis by PTH and calcitonin, and distinguish synovial joint classifications."

## Module 3: Movement & Neurophysiology
*Goal: Master neuromuscular contraction physiology and membrane biophysics.*

### Lesson 1.6: The Muscular System & Contraction Physiology
* **Concept:** Microscopic anatomy of a sarcomere (actin, myosin, troponin, tropomyosin, Z-discs, A/I bands), events at the neuromuscular junction (NMJ), excitation-contraction coupling, sliding filament mechanism, muscle metabolism (creatine phosphate, anaerobic/aerobic), muscle actions, and relationships (agonists, antagonists, synergists, fixators).
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** Analyzing how Botox blocks acetylcholine release at the NMJ causing flaccid paralysis vs. how tetanus toxin causes spastic paralysis (lockjaw).
* **Interactive Target:** `sarcomere-sequencing-game`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the chronological steps of excitation-contraction coupling and the sliding filament mechanism of muscle contraction, highlighting agonists and antagonists."

### Lesson 1.7: Neurophysiology & Excitable Membranes
* **Concept:** Division of neural cells (neurons vs. neuroglia: astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia, Schwann cells), electrochemical gradients, resting membrane potential, biophysics of graded vs. action potentials (voltage-gated Na+/K+ channels, depolarization, repolarization, hyperpolarization), and synaptic transmission.
* **Clinical/Real-World Hook:** How altering extracellular potassium concentrations (hyperkalemia) blocks action potential propagation and causes cardiac arrest.
* **Interactive Target:** `action-potential-ion-grapher`
* **Feynman Prompt:** "Explain the biophysical mechanisms that generate graded potentials and action potentials, detailing the role of voltage-gated sodium and potassium channels."
